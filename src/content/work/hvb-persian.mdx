## The problem

Sentiment in Persian product reviews is harder to classify than it looks. Reviews are written informally, labeled
data is scarce, and negation and sarcasm weaken models that rely on simple polarity cues. A transformer fine-tuned on
reviews reads context well, but its decisions are hard to inspect. A lexicon is transparent and cheap, but blind to
context.

## The approach

HVB-Persian uses both. A ParsBERT classifier is fine-tuned on the reviews and produces class scores from the whole
sentence. In parallel, VADER produces a polarity score from word-level rules. The two are combined by a weighted
score fusion, and the weights are tuned on validation data rather than fixed by hand.

Pairing a learned representation with an explicit, rule-based polarity signal is meant to make the pipeline more
reliable, and easier to interpret, than either part alone.

## Data and results

The reviews come from two Persian platforms, Digikala and Snappfood. I prepared both and merged them into a combined
dataset of about 84,000 reviews.

## Where it came from

This paper sits on earlier, smaller experiments on the same question, each public:

- **[Classical baselines](https://github.com/SoroushJaberi/SentimentPredModels).** Naive Bayes, Linear SVC, SGD and
  Random Forest classifiers on TF-IDF features, compared on accuracy, precision, recall and F1. All four land between
  0.857 and 0.862 accuracy.
- **[Persian sentiment with FastText](https://github.com/SoroushJaberi/Persian-sentiment-analysis-using-FastText-Model-NLP-).**
  A three-class classifier (positive, negative, neutral) for Persian text, with 86% accuracy.
