## The problem

Outlining a tumor by hand on every slice of a CT scan is slow, and the thing you are looking for is tiny. In a lung
scan most voxels are not tumor, so a model can reach a very low loss by predicting “nothing” everywhere. Getting a
segmentation model to learn the small region that matters is mostly a problem of imbalance.

## Two pipelines

Both models are U-Nets: an encoder that shrinks the image while widening the features, a decoder that restores the
resolution, and skip connections that carry detail across.

**Liver.** The liver model works on whole volumes. It takes the 2D U-Net and makes it three-dimensional: 3D
convolutions and pooling, trilinear upsampling, and three output channels for background, liver and tumor. The
original Decathlon set is over 26 GB, so the scans were resampled to 256 × 256 × Z, which brings it to about 2.5 GB.
105 subjects were used for training and 13 for testing.

<Note label="Why oversample">
  Typically there is one weight per class. Here that means one weight for slices without a tumor and one for slices
  with a tumor, assigned to every sample in the dataset.
</Note>

**Lung.** Lung tumors are often very small, so the lung model is trained slice by slice with oversampling. A
`WeightedRandomSampler` draws slices that contain a tumor more often, so that the network cannot settle on the
trivial solution of outputting zero for every voxel. The lung model reaches a Dice score of 0.80 on validation data.

Both were trained on a single consumer GPU, a GTX 1050 Ti. The notebooks are public; the lung repository also
includes rendered result pages.

## What it led to

These projects are the practical foundation for a manuscript I am writing with Dr. Amineh Amini, *A Systematic
Mapping Study on Deep Learning-Based Biomedical Image Segmentation Techniques*. It surveys architectures across biomedical imaging modalities and supervised,
semi-supervised and unsupervised methods, with attention to evaluation metrics and clinical relevance, and sets out
the open problems in 3D architectures and standardized evaluation protocols. It is in preparation.
